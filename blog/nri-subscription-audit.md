# The NRI Subscription Audit: Why You're Paying for Twice as Many Streaming Services

If you're an NRI — Indian living in the US, UK, Canada, Australia, or anywhere outside India — there's a high chance you're paying for **double the streaming services you need**. This is structural, not a personal failure. Here's why and what to do.

## The structural problem

NRIs typically maintain two "lives" of subscriptions:

**Indian side (paid via Indian credit card / UPI / family's card):**
- Hotstar (for cricket, Bollywood)
- JioCinema or SonyLIV (for IPL or specific shows)
- A Jio/Airtel plan that bundles streaming
- Maybe Audible India

**US/Foreign side (paid in USD/GBP/CAD):**
- Netflix
- Disney+ (the foreign version, not Hotstar)
- Hulu / Prime Video
- HBO Max
- Apple TV+, Apple Music, Apple iCloud
- ChatGPT or other AI tools

Total streaming spend for the typical NRI: **$60-$120/month equivalent**, which is ₹5,000-₹10,000/month.

## The duplicates almost everyone has

### 1. Disney+ Hotstar (India) + Disney+ (US/UK)

Different services with overlapping content. You only need one. Hotstar has more Indian content; Disney+ has more Marvel/Star Wars early releases. Pick based on what you actually watch.

### 2. Amazon Prime India + Amazon Prime US

Most NRIs maintain both. The Indian one for shipping to family, the US one for personal use. Often the cheapest duplicate to live with (~$15+₹1,500/year), but if family doesn't actively order through you, cancel the Indian one.

### 3. Spotify + Apple Music + YouTube Premium

Three music services that do the same thing. You picked one, migrated, never canceled the others. **$30+/month wasted.**

### 4. Netflix India + Netflix US

Mostly happens during the transition year — you didn't cancel the Indian one when you moved. Easy fix.

### 5. iCloud+ (Apple ID India) + iCloud+ (Apple ID US)

If you use two Apple IDs (one for the India App Store, one for the US App Store), you may have two iCloud+ subscriptions. Most people only need one.

## The currency conversion trap

NRIs often think Indian subscriptions are "negligible" because they're in rupees. **They're not.**

A ₹299/month Hotstar = $3.60/month. That feels small.

But you probably also pay:
- ₹999 Cult.fit (you don't visit India enough) = $12/month
- ₹1,499/year Times Prime = $1.50/month
- ₹199 Audible India = $2.40/month
- ₹699/year Swiggy One (you don't even live in India) = $0.70/month
- ₹1,675/month Adobe Creative Cloud India pricing = $20/month

Adds up to **$40+/month** in "small" Indian subscriptions on top of your US bills.

## The "for my parents" trap

Many NRIs have subscriptions registered to their email that are actually being used by parents/siblings in India. Common cases:

- Hotstar for parents to watch cricket
- Amazon Prime India for shipping to home
- Netflix for siblings

These aren't waste — they're family support. But:

1. **Track them separately** so you know what you're paying for whom.
2. **Don't double up** — if your sibling has their own Netflix, you can cancel yours.
3. **Consider Family plans** — Apple One Family, Spotify Family, Amazon Prime household sharing can consolidate.

## FAQ

**I have subscriptions on three different emails — what should I do?**
Scan each separately. Most paid services bill to one specific email — usually personal, but sometimes a work or India-specific account.

**My Indian credit card auto-converts to USD on US receipts — is that a duplicate?**
No. That's the same subscription, just billed in a different currency. Duplicates are two **different** services that do the same thing.

**Can I use Indian subscriptions while abroad?**
Hotstar, JioCinema, SonyLIV often have geo-restrictions. Many NRIs pay for Hotstar but can't watch it without a VPN — defeating the purpose.
